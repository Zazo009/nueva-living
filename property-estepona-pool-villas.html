Overview

Nine villas, six listed

This is a development of nine villas with four and five bedrooms in Estepona. Six are listed with a size and a price, from EUR 2,300,000 to EUR 2,680,000. The developer’s infosheet calls the villas key ready, lists the building licence and the first occupation licence as in place and the end of construction as finished, and gives large plots of up to 1,000 sqm, a private outdoor pool, a large basement, private parking for two cars, underfloor heating and a south-east orientation.

The quality specification describes a reinforced concrete structure, Daikin aerothermal heating, cooling and hot water, a Control4 smart home system, Neff appliances and wall-hung Duravit sanitaryware. The documents give no completion date and no floorplans, and no plot size for any single villa.

What the villas are built with

The developer’s own quality specification, summarised by part of the villa.

Floors and walls
  • Nexus White stoneware tiles of 60 by 60 cm for the common areas of the house, the stairs and the basement bedrooms and bathrooms, oak floorboards in the ground-floor and first-floor bedrooms, ceramic skirting in the basements, plasterboard false ceilings throughout and acrylic paint inside.

Kitchen
  • Kitchen furniture by Danespan, a Silestone Calacatta Eternal Gold worktop, and appliances by the German brand Neff. The specification does not list which appliances.
Bathrooms
  • Wall-hung Duravit sanitaryware with Tece concealed cisterns, ASM mixers, mirrors backlit by an LED strip, and porcelain wall tiling by Cifre and Grespania.
  • In the main bathroom a bathtub (Sanyc Torino 170 or Trento 150), a wall-hung bidet, a built-in two-outlet shower kit with a 200 mm shower head, and a polished Grespania porcelain countertop with a wall-hung drawer unit. The secondary bathrooms have solid surface countertops and a telescopic shower column.
Climate and installations
  • A Daikin Altherma aerothermal system that supplies heating, cooling and hot water, with the outdoor unit on the roof and ducted inverter indoor units in the false ceilings of bathrooms or dressing rooms, water underfloor heating throughout the house and part of the basement, and forced ventilation as the Technical Building Code requires.
  • Jung F-40 LS electrical fittings, LED spotlights with movement sensors in the transit areas, a Control4 smart home system with a 10 inch built-in touch screen, a Fermax Smile video intercom, Wi-Fi throughout the house and a pre-installed wired alarm. The maximum permitted power supply is 14,490 W.
Pool and garden
  • A pool with a sand filter and a salt chlorinator, 24 W 12 V LED spotlights, an overflow channel round the edge, and the machinery in a hidden room under the deck, reached through a metal cover.
  • A finished garden irrigation network with programmers and a pressure unit, a drinking-water cistern and an irrigation cistern, and plots separated by electro-welded mesh, retaining walls and a planted enclosure.

Architecture

White volumes, open to the garden

The photographs show white-rendered villas on several levels, with deep overhangs, flat roofs and pitched tile roofs, glass balustrades on the terraces, and sliding glass walls that open the living areas onto a covered terrace, a paved terrace and the pool.

The infosheet describes the design only as top notch. The notes below are the developer’s own description of how the villas are built.

  • Structure — A reinforced concrete slab foundation set by the geotechnical report, a reinforced concrete frame with waffle or solid slabs, and reinforced concrete retaining walls.
  • Facade — Hollow brick with an inner and an outer render, an air chamber and 5 cm of projected polyurethane foam insulation, finished in waterproof cement render and silicone resin paint, with some walls clad in ceramic tiles.
  • Glazing — Cortizo aluminium in the COR 60 series, with the COR 4500 elevating series for the larger windows, thermally broken and lacquered grey, with Planitherm double glazing and 8+8 or 10+10 safety glass railings.

FAQ

Common buyer questions

Can foreigners buy property in Spain?

Yes. There are no restrictions on non-Spanish nationals buying property in Spain, whether as a resident or non-resident.

What is an NIE number and do I need one?

An NIE (Numero de Identificacion de Extranjero) is a tax ID number required for any property purchase in Spain by a non-Spanish national. Nueva Living can guide you through obtaining one before you reserve.

What costs should I budget for on top of the purchase price?

Buyers typically budget for transfer tax or VAT, notary fees, land registry fees and legal fees on top of the purchase price. Nueva Living provides a full, current cost breakdown for your chosen residence before you reserve.

Can I get a mortgage in Spain as a non-resident?

Many Spanish banks offer mortgages to non-resident buyers, typically financing a portion of the purchase price. Exact terms depend on the bank and your personal financial profile.

What is the difference between off-plan and completed properties?

Off-plan means the development is still under construction and is usually sold with staged payments through to completion. A completed property is ready to view and move into now.

How does the reservation and payment process work?

Reservation and payment structures vary by development and are set out in full before you reserve. Nueva Living reconfirms the current schedule for your chosen residence at every step.

Can I rent out the property after purchase?

This depends on the individual development and local regulations, which can vary by community and municipality. Nueva Living will confirm the specific rules for a development before you reserve.

Do I need a lawyer?

Yes, we strongly recommend independent legal representation for any property purchase in Spain. Nueva Living can put you in touch with independent lawyers experienced in Costa del Sol property.
