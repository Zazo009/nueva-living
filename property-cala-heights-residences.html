Overview

Fifty homes, key ready

Cala Heights Residences is a gated, key-ready community of 50 apartments in La Cala de Mijas, on the Costa del Sol. The developer’s infosheet lists homes of one, 1½, two and three bedrooms, and a community with a pool area, an infinity pool, a padel court, an outdoor gym and a children’s playground.

Five homes are listed as available: two of 1½ bedrooms and three of two bedrooms, from 87.64 to 102.06 sqm, at EUR 680,000 to EUR 930,000. The infosheet lists construction as started in the second quarter of 2021 and finished in the second quarter of 2025, with the building licence in place; it leaves the first occupation licence blank.

This is the earlier phase of a scheme whose later phase, blocks six and seven, is on this site as La Noria Golf Residences. The site plan we hold numbers five blocks, 01 to 05.

Quality specifications

What the homes are built with

The developer’s own building specification, summarised by part of the home.

Floors and walls
  • Porcelain stoneware floors throughout the home (Plank Grey by Newker or similar), and non-slip porcelain stoneware on the terraces (Land Grey by Newker or similar).
  • Bathroom walls tiled in Citi White at the washbasin and Lava Grey at the shower, by Newker or similar, and the rest painted; toilet rooms tiled in Citi White. Plain plastic paint on the walls and ceilings of the homes.
Doors and wardrobes
  • A security entrance door with anti-lever hinges, polyurethane insulation, a security lock and a spyhole.
  • Interior doors in white lacquered wood with stainless-steel fittings, hinged or sliding according to the plans, and modular wardrobes lacquered white, with a luggage shelf and a hanging rail.
Kitchen
  • A fully fitted kitchen with upper and lower units and a contemporary worktop, with an oven, a ceramic hob, an extractor hood, a fridge and a dishwasher, and a stainless-steel sink with a single-lever mixer tap.
  • The infosheet adds that the appliances are by Bosch and that the kitchen has no microwave.
Bathrooms
  • White glazed porcelain sanitaryware by Roca or similar, with chrome single-lever mixers with ceramic cartridges by Hansgrohe or similar.
  • The main bathroom has a Roca Cratos shower tray, a Hansgrohe Croma E Showerpipe 280 thermostatic column and a screen. The secondary bathroom has a Roca Cratos shower tray with a Crometta E Showerpipe 240 column, and/or a Roca Contesa bath, with a screen. A toilet room has a Roca The Gap washbasin and toilet.
Climate and hot water
  • Ducted air conditioning in each home, with a single thermostat for the living room and bedrooms, served by an aerothermal heat pump.
  • Hot water from the aerothermal heat pump, with an individual storage tank in each home, and a hot-water underfloor heating system in the living room and bedrooms. The infosheet lists air conditioning, hot and cold, and underfloor heating throughout, but not in the bathrooms.
Electrics and technology
  • An electrical installation to low-voltage regulations, light points on terraces and common areas, and a socket and a water tap on the main terrace.
  • Telecommunications sockets in the lounges, bedrooms and kitchen, an outside TV socket, and a video entry phone with an individual monitor in each home.
Parking, lift and common areas
  • A storage room and a parking space as inseparable annexes of each home, and garage doors that open automatically by remote control.
  • A lift for six people with automatic doors, going down to the garage level.
  • A closed, private community with adults’ and children’s pools with night lighting, a padel court (the English column says one court, the Spanish column says courts), a children’s play area, garden areas and private gardens for residents.

Source: the developer’s building specification (Memoria de calidades, Spanish and English columns), whose file is dated 31 August 2021, so it describes what was specified in 2021. It says the project management may change the specification and replace items with similar ones without a loss of quality. Brands are given as “or similar”. It lists no loose furniture, and it does not mention an infinity pool or a multipurpose room, which the 2026 infosheet lists.

Architecture

White terraces, stepping down the slope

The photographs show white blocks whose terraces step back level by level, with glass balustrades and, on the upper levels, slatted pergola shading, on a slope above a road. The developer’s building specification describes how they are built.

The specification is dated 31 August 2021, and it allows the project management to replace materials with similar ones without a loss of quality, so we confirm what was built.

  • Structure — A reinforced concrete structure with waffle or reinforced slabs on a slab or footings, with a pile screen and micropiles to stabilise the slope.
  • Facade — A double-leaf facade: a perforated-brick outer leaf, sprayed insulation, an air chamber and an inner leaf of dry lining on a metal frame with mineral wool. The outside is continuous mortar render with a paint finish, combined with stone-type panels.
  • Glazing — Lacquered extruded aluminium windows by Strugal or Cortizo, or similar, with a thermal break and thermal-acoustic double glazing, and air inlets for ventilation.
  • Terraces and roofs — Terrace balustrades are a mix of solid wall and glass, according to the elevations, and terraces are paved in non-slip porcelain stoneware. Roofs are insulated and waterproofed, either walk-on terraces or non-accessible roofs.
  • Walls between homes — Acoustic ceramic brick, lined on both sides with dry lining and mineral wool, between homes and between homes and common areas.

FAQ

Common buyer questions

Can foreigners buy property in Spain?

Yes. There are no restrictions on non-Spanish nationals buying property in Spain, whether as a resident or non-resident.

What is an NIE number and do I need one?

An NIE (Numero de Identificacion de Extranjero) is a tax ID number required for any property purchase in Spain by a non-Spanish national. Nueva Living can guide you through obtaining one before you reserve.

What costs should I budget for on top of the purchase price?

Buyers typically budget for transfer tax or VAT, notary fees, land registry fees and legal fees on top of the purchase price. Nueva Living provides a full, current cost breakdown for your chosen residence before you reserve.

Can I get a mortgage in Spain as a non-resident?

Many Spanish banks offer mortgages to non-resident buyers, typically financing a portion of the purchase price. Exact terms depend on the bank and your personal financial profile.

What is the difference between off-plan and completed properties?

Off-plan means the development is still under construction and is usually sold with staged payments through to completion. A completed property is ready to view and move into now.

How does the reservation and payment process work?

Reservation and payment structures vary by development and are set out in full before you reserve. Nueva Living reconfirms the current schedule for your chosen residence at every step.

Can I rent out the property after purchase?

This depends on the individual development and local regulations, which can vary by community and municipality. Nueva Living will confirm the specific rules for a development before you reserve.

Do I need a lawyer?

Yes, we strongly recommend independent legal representation for any property purchase in Spain. Nueva Living can put you in touch with independent lawyers experienced in Costa del Sol property.
