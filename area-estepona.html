Costa del Sol Area Guide Estepona, area by area: where to buy and what it costs

A walkable town, long seafront and strong new-build supply, with a calmer pace than central Marbella.

Living in Estepona

Coastal living with room to breathe

The main choice is between the walkable town centre, beachside communities and the New Golden Mile. The latter offers newer residential projects between Estepona and Marbella, but daily life is generally more car-dependent.

01

Best For

Buyers seeking a practical coastal base with more value range than central Marbella.

02

Town or New Golden Mile

The centre offers walkability; newer coastal communities offer pools, space and managed grounds.

03

Daily Life

The old town, promenade, schools, healthcare and shops remain active throughout the year.

04

Access

The A-7 connects Estepona with Marbella, Gibraltar and Málaga Airport; journey times vary with traffic.

Local Knowledge

What makes Estepona different

Estepona has changed more than any other town on this coast in the last decade -- worth understanding before you compare it to Marbella.

01

Calle Real and the flower streets

A years-long municipal push to repaint facades and hang flower pots on every balcony earned Estepona the nickname “Garden of the Costa del Sol.” It is one of the few old towns here that feels cared for rather than preserved for tourists.

02

The Orchidarium

A striking glass botanical garden in the town centre, home to thousands of orchid species -- an unusual cultural anchor for a coastal town this size.

03

The port and fish market

Still a working fishing harbour, not just a marina. The daily catch shows up on the same evening's restaurant menus along the front.

04

The New Golden Mile

The stretch running east from Estepona toward Marbella, including Cancelada, is where most of the town's new-development activity sits -- close to the coast without central Marbella's density or prices.

Where to Buy

Five settings, priced differently

01

The town and its edges

Estepona's own centre, with the old town, the promenade, schools, healthcare and shops active through the year. Las Mesas sits on the eastern edge between the sea and the Sierra Bermeja; Bahía Dorada is on the western edge, around a ten-minute walk from Arroyo Vaquero beach.

02

The New Golden Mile

The coastal stretch between Estepona and San Pedro Alcántara, and where most of the newer residential projects are. Cancelada, Bel Air and La Resina all sit on it, with direct access to the coast road and Marbella and Estepona both within reach.

03

New developments in Cancelada

A quiet residential area on the New Golden Mile between Estepona and Marbella, with direct access to the coast road and the beach close by. Estepona and Puerto Banús are each 12 km away and Marbella 21 km. The new developments here range from low-rise apartments and penthouses to two- and three-bedroom ground-floor homes with a garden or large terraces.

04

El Campanario and the golf urbanisations

An established golf urbanisation on the New Golden Mile with the clubhouse inside it and the coast a short drive south. Suits buyers who want managed grounds and a course on the doorstep rather than a town centre.

05

The beachside

Guadalobon sits between the Mediterranean and the Sierra Bermeja, close to the beach and Valle Romano Golf, with Marbella around twenty to twenty-five minutes away. Beachside pricing runs above the municipal average.

FAQ

What buyers ask us about Estepona

How much does property cost in Estepona?

The average asking price for resale homes was €4,329 per m² in June 2026, against €5,608 for Marbella on the same measure and month. Beachside and newly built homes are generally priced above the local average rather than at it, so treat the figure as a starting point for the municipality, not a guide to any one address.

Should I buy in Estepona town or on the New Golden Mile?

The town centre gives you walkability: the old town, the promenade, schools, healthcare and shops stay active through the year. The New Golden Mile, between Estepona and Marbella, is where the newer residential projects are, with pools, space and managed grounds, but daily life there is generally more car-dependent.

How do you get to Málaga airport from Estepona?

By car, on the A-7, which connects Estepona with Marbella, Gibraltar and the airport. Journey times vary considerably with traffic and with where in the municipality you are, so the drive is worth timing from the specific address rather than from the town centre.

Is Estepona quieter than Marbella?

Generally yes. It has a calmer pace than central Marbella while keeping a working town behind it rather than a resort strip, which is what makes it practical outside the season as well as during it.

Is there much new-build in Estepona?

Estepona has one of the stronger new-build pipelines on this coast, spread between the town, the beachside communities and the New Golden Mile. The developments we currently hold information on are listed above, with real availability rather than a general search.

Estepona or Casares — which should I look at?

Casares sits immediately west and is where buyers often look for new-build near the coast at lower pricing, but it has limited everyday services of its own and Estepona is where most Casares residents shop, eat out and see a doctor. If you want the services on your doorstep rather than fifteen minutes away, that argues for Estepona.
