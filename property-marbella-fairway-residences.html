Overview

A hundred and four apartments, 4 listed

This is a gated development of 104 apartments with three or four bedrooms in east Marbella, on the first line of a golf course. It stands on two plots, numbered 2.1 and 2.3 on the developer’s site plan, which shows 16 numbered blocks on plot 2.1 and 10 on plot 2.3. The infosheet puts four apartments in each block, which matches the 104. 4 homes are listed with a size and a price, all of them with three bedrooms.

The infosheet and the quality specification give every apartment a private pool with salt chlorination, in the garden of the ground-floor homes or on the sun deck above the first floor, two parking spaces and en-suite bedrooms. The infosheet lists the building licence as in place, the first-occupation licence as granted and the development as key ready, with construction from Q4 2023 to Q4 2025.

Quality specifications

What the homes are built with

The developer’s own quality specification, summarised by part of the home.

Floors, walls and ceilings
  • Rectified porcelain tiles in 90 by 90 cm format throughout the home, bathrooms included, and the same format in a non-slip finish on the terraces, with white-lacquered wooden skirting.
  • In the bathrooms, rectified wall tiles in 40 by 120 cm and 120 by 270 cm formats, with smooth plastic paint on the other walls. Plasterboard false ceilings throughout, recessed along the windows in the living room and the main bedrooms, and wood-texture composite ceilings on the terraces.
Doors and wardrobes
  • An armoured entrance door with a security lock and five anchor points, floor-to-ceiling white-lacquered interior doors with an acoustic seal, and matt chrome or stainless-steel fittings.
  • Built-in wardrobes with hinged doors lacquered to match, lined inside and fitted with drawers, a luggage shelf and a hanging rail.
Kitchen
  • Fitted kitchens with large-capacity upper and lower units in a laminate finish, a compact quartz worktop and splashback, and a stainless-steel sink with a low-flow single-lever tap with a pull-out hose.
  • Siemens appliances: an ultra-slim extractor hood, an electric oven and a microwave in a glass-finish column, an induction hob, an integrated fridge, a stainless-steel washing machine and an integrated dishwasher, with LED lighting between the units.
Bathrooms
  • Porcelanosa fittings: wall-hung Krion washbasins, double in the main bathrooms and single in the others, with vanity units, resin shower trays, and wall-hung toilets with a dual flush and a slow-close seat.
  • Concealed thermostatic shower taps with a ceiling rain head in the main bathrooms, single-lever shower mixers in the others, and wall-mounted basin taps. Mirrors and shower screens are included, and loudspeakers with a Bluetooth connection are fitted in the main bathroom and the kitchen.
Climate and installations
  • Heating and cooling by an aerothermal heat pump through ducts in the false ceiling, with Airzone zoning by room and a heat-recovery unit, underfloor heating throughout the home, and hot water from the same aerothermal system.
  • Cross-linked polyethylene water pipes, a general shut-off valve and separate ones for the kitchen and the bathrooms, water points on all terraces and private outdoor spaces, and a high level of electrification with more electrical and telecommunication sockets than the regulations require.
Security and access
  • An automatic colour video door entry and a closed-circuit television system, a security control lodge in the common areas, and mobile-phone coverage in the garage on level -1.
  • A garage door with a motor and a remote control, a continuous polished-concrete garage floor, fire protection to the regulations, security doors on the storerooms in the parking areas, and a charging installation for electric vehicles in the garage spaces, according to the regulations.
Private pools and gardens
  • A private skimmer pool with a salt chlorinator in every home, in the garden of the ground-floor homes and on the sun deck above the first floor.
  • Ground-floor homes also have a private outdoor area laid to lawn, with pre-installation for drip irrigation. The communal gardens use low-water and native plants with irrigation.
Common areas
  • Lifts with entry from every floor and a direct link to the garage levels, with automatic doors, overload detection and a telephone connection, and an accessible cabin. Entrances and staircases are lit, with presence detectors and timers to cut the use of electricity.
  • The common areas are designed to keep the community expenses as low as they can be, with installations of the highest quality and solutions that cut maintenance costs.

Architecture

Terraced blocks, timber and white render

The photographs show white blocks of stepped terraces with timber-coloured slatted panels and glazed fronts, gardens with a long narrow pool at ground level and pools on the roof decks, in a landscaped setting beside the golf course. The quality specification describes rendered façades with ceramic and wood-texture composite elements placed to bring out the volumes.

The site plan and the specification are indicative: the developer says that the plan is not final and that it may make the changes that technical or legal requirements demand.

  • Structure — Reinforced concrete, with the foundations laid in line with the geotechnical report, under the Spanish Technical Building Code.
  • Facade — Rendered façades with ceramic and wood-texture composite elements placed to emphasise the volumes.
  • Glazing — Lacquered aluminium frames with a thermal break, in a minimalist design, with sliding, hinged or tilt-and-turn openings by location, and Climalit double glazing with a low-emissivity coating and solar control where the sun requires it.
  • Roofs — Flat inverted roofs with a double bituminous membrane and insulation, finished in gravel where they are not accessible and in non-slip ceramic tiles where they are.

FAQ

Common buyer questions

Can foreigners buy property in Spain?

Yes. There are no restrictions on non-Spanish nationals buying property in Spain, whether as a resident or non-resident.

What is an NIE number and do I need one?

An NIE (Numero de Identificacion de Extranjero) is a tax ID number required for any property purchase in Spain by a non-Spanish national. Nueva Living can guide you through obtaining one before you reserve.

What costs should I budget for on top of the purchase price?

Buyers typically budget for transfer tax or VAT, notary fees, land registry fees and legal fees on top of the purchase price. Nueva Living provides a full, current cost breakdown for your chosen residence before you reserve.

Can I get a mortgage in Spain as a non-resident?

Many Spanish banks offer mortgages to non-resident buyers, typically financing a portion of the purchase price. Exact terms depend on the bank and your personal financial profile.

What is the difference between off-plan and completed properties?

Off-plan means the development is still under construction and is usually sold with staged payments through to completion. A completed property is ready to view and move into now.

How does the reservation and payment process work?

Reservation and payment structures vary by development and are set out in full before you reserve. Nueva Living reconfirms the current schedule for your chosen residence at every step.

Can I rent out the property after purchase?

This depends on the individual development and local regulations, which can vary by community and municipality. Nueva Living will confirm the specific rules for a development before you reserve.

Do I need a lawyer?

Yes, we strongly recommend independent legal representation for any property purchase in Spain. Nueva Living can put you in touch with independent lawyers experienced in Costa del Sol property.
