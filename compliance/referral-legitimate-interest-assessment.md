# Legitimate interest assessment — referral introductions

**Controller** LIORA LIVING SL. (NIF B88827472), trading as Nueva Living
**Processing** Contacting a person whose name and contact details were supplied by a third party
through the referral form at /referrals.html
**Lawful basis relied on** Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, legitimate interest
**Prepared** 6 October 2026 · to be reviewed by legal counsel before the programme pays out

---

## 1. Purpose test — is there a legitimate interest?

Responding to an introduction made by someone who knows both parties, in order to offer a
buying advisory service the referrer believed would be useful. This is a commercial interest,
which the GDPR recognises as capable of being legitimate (Recital 47 names direct marketing as
an example, without making it automatic).

The interest is specific and short-lived: it covers one first contact, not an ongoing
relationship, unless the person chooses to continue.

## 2. Necessity test — is the processing necessary for it?

There is no way to respond to an introduction without processing the introduced person's
contact details. The alternative — a referral code the buyer uses to contact us themselves —
removes the need entirely, and is the structure we would prefer. Until that is built, the
processing is necessary for the purpose as the programme currently works.

Only what is needed is processed: a name, one contact channel, and whatever the referrer chose
to say about what they are looking for. No data is bought, enriched or combined.

## 3. Balancing test — does the interest override the person's rights?

| In favour of the processing | Against |
| --- | --- |
| The referrer knows the person and judged the introduction useful | The person did not ask to be contacted and may not know their details were shared |
| One contact only; stops immediately on request | Being approached by a business about a large purchase is intrusive if unwanted |
| The ambassador confirms at submission that they have the person's permission | That confirmation is the referrer's word, not the person's own consent |
| No profiling, no enrichment, no sale of data | Budget and timing may be mentioned by the referrer — financial information about someone absent |

**Conclusion.** The balance holds only with the safeguards below. Without them it does not: a
cold approach using a third party's financial details, with no explanation of where they came
from, would not be within a person's reasonable expectations.

## 4. Safeguards — these are conditions, not extras

1. The first contact carries the Article 14 information in the message itself, including who
   gave us the details. Not a link to this page: in the message.
2. The right to object is stated separately and plainly in that first message, as Art. 21(4)
   requires, not folded into a privacy notice.
3. One contact. If there is no reply, there is no second approach.
4. An objection is honoured immediately and the record suppressed rather than deleted, so the
   same introduction cannot be re-entered later.
5. Referrer-supplied details about budget or timing are not retained after the first
   conversation unless the person continues it with us themselves.

## 5. Spanish overlay — LSSI-CE

Art. 21 of Ley 34/2002 (LSSI-CE) requires prior consent for unsolicited commercial
communications by email or SMS where there is no prior contractual relationship. An introduced
person has none. Legitimate interest under the GDPR makes the *processing* lawful; it does not
by itself make an unsolicited commercial *email* lawful under LSSI.

This is the strongest argument for changing the flow so the buyer makes contact first. Until
then, first contact should be made by the referrer introducing both parties — for example a
message or call where the person is present — rather than by Nueva Living emailing someone who
has never heard of us.
